# Research Vietnam-specific context

Use this skill when a translation or editorial proposal depends on Vietnamese law, regulations, public policy, or cultural context.

## Research process

1. Turn each item to verify into a specific question. Separate legal claims, factual claims, and cultural/editorial judgments; they require different evidence.
2. Prefer authoritative, current sources: Vietnamese legal text and official government or regulator publications for law; reputable primary research and well-sourced reporting for social context. Avoid relying on search snippets, unsourced summaries, or a single secondary source for consequential claims.
3. Verify that a source supports the exact claim. For laws and regulations, record the document name/number, relevant article or clause, issuing authority, effective date and status (including amendments or replacement) where available. Distinguish enacted requirements from proposals, guidance, and common practice.
4. Provide a working source link and the date checked for each material finding. Quote only the minimum needed; summarize in your own words. If authoritative sources conflict or current status cannot be established, say so explicitly and do not resolve the uncertainty by guessing.
5. Treat culture as diverse and contextual. Avoid presenting a regional, generational, or personal practice as universal to Vietnam; identify the audience and context, and label recommendations as editorial judgment rather than fact.
6. Treat localization as required for publishable content in this repository: replace China-specific laws, regulations, agencies, public services, emergency numbers, government programs, monetary values/prices, statistics, and other jurisdiction-dependent facts with current Vietnamese equivalents only when verified. Do not mechanically convert CNY to VND, map one country's law to another, or substitute a Vietnamese phone number/program by guesswork.
7. If no reliable Vietnamese equivalent or sufficiently current local data can be established, explicitly mark the item as pending research or omit the actionable detail; retain it as source-context only when useful and clearly labelled. For topics with sparse Vietnam-specific evidence, international studies or statistics may fill the evidence gap when their country/region, population, year, source, and limitations are stated; they must not be framed as Vietnam-specific data or used alone to assert local law, public-service details, or locally applicable clinical guidance. Report material differences and the source-check date.
8. Report findings separately from the translation. Suggest localized wording or examples only when they preserve the source's intent; explain material deviations and request review where appropriate.

## Output

For each research question, return:
- **Kết luận:** concise finding and confidence/limitations.
- **Nguồn:** title or issuing body, applicable provision if relevant, link, and checked date.
- **Tác động biên tập:** whether the Vietnamese version should change, add a note, or retain the source wording.

This research is informational, not legal advice. For consequential decisions, disputed interpretation, or individual circumstances, recommend confirmation with a qualified Vietnamese legal professional or relevant authority.
